# Visual judgment

The job is not to maximize rules. It is to make coherent visual decisions.

## Evaluate relationships
Never judge font, color, spacing, image or animation in isolation. Ask what it does to hierarchy, character, clarity and the neighboring elements.

## Intentional rule breaking
A convention may be broken when the agent can name the convention, explain the intended gain, identify the cost and control that cost. Accidental violation is not creativity.

## Squint test
Blur or mentally squint at the viewport. Major masses, focal point and CTA should remain understandable.

## Five-second test
After a brief look, the viewer should understand what kind of service this is, what the dominant promise is and where the main action lives. Visual intrigue may delay detail, not basic orientation.

## Distance test
Review at normal phone distance and desktop distance. A composition that only looks good zoomed into a screenshot is not finished.

## Comparison test
Place the actual render beside strong current references. Do not ask whether Spline is clean; ask where it is weaker in typography, composition, image world, rhythm, craft and memorability.

## Originality test
Remove logo and copy mentally. If the remaining composition could belong to any generic SaaS or automotive template, the visual concept is weak.

## Coherence test
Every system should support the concept. Too many good ideas can make a bad design when they compete.

## Commercial test
Beauty must increase attention, trust, comprehension or desire to proceed. Spectacle that hides the request path is failure.

## Restraint test
After the concept is established, remove nonessential effects. Strong art direction often becomes more distinctive when generic decoration is deleted.

## Render truth
Never award visual PASS from code, tokens, a design document or successful build. Inspect the actual page at mobile and desktop after implementation.
