# Imagery and graphic language

Define a visual world rather than selecting images independently: subject, camera distance, perspective, lighting, contrast, background, crop, texture, color treatment and relationship to typography.

Real Spline assets outrank generic stock when credible. Never fabricate facilities, staff, inventory, suppliers, customers or proof. Generated or abstract visuals must read as expressive art direction rather than documentary evidence.

Crop communicates hierarchy and energy. Macro crops can create tactility, wider views create context, and partial objects can imply continuation. Do not crop away meaning merely for decoration.

Consistency can come from light, palette, texture, perspective or treatment while framing and scale still vary.

Lines, borders, radii, icons, arrows, labels, textures, masks and shapes form one graphic grammar. Repeated motifs need structural, semantic or expressive logic. Avoid unrelated fashionable motifs.

Texture can add physicality but must not reduce legibility. Prefer material cues connected to the concept over generic grain/noise.

Review whether imagery belongs to one world, crops have intent, graphic motifs have a coherent grammar, and visual material reinforces the concept instead of acting as filler.
