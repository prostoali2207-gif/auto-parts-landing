# Spline — Exploration Evaluation / Elimination Round

Status: decision gate after A1–A6 / C1–C6 exploration. No new image generation in this round.

## Evidence used

- Bosch's historic mark was explicitly required to be simple, distinctive, internationally understandable and engravable on even the smallest products/spare parts. Lesson for Spline: small-scale recognition and structural simplicity outrank presentation effects.
- Brembo's 2022 identity refresh optimized symbol/lettering coherence and readability at any size, especially digital. Lesson: custom geometry must not damage word recognition.
- Pentagram's Vroom identity deliberately avoided obvious car-related visual cleverness and let the name/wordmark do the work. Lesson: Spline does not need a literal automotive pictogram.
- Pentagram's Jetour G Series centers a refined wordmark/custom typeface around restrained geometry and precision engineering. Lesson: an engineered wordmark can carry automotive credibility without a separate emblem.

## Evaluation criteria

1. SPLINE readability at first glance
2. Distinctiveness without gimmick
3. Mechanical-fit idea is understandable after explanation but not required for reading
4. Small-size / mobile-header robustness
5. Monochrome robustness
6. Avoidance of generic tuning / gaming / crypto / SaaS aesthetics
7. Ease of building a coherent identity system later
8. Practicality for a young UAE auto-parts business

## A-series — wordmark elimination

### A1 — KEEP / finalist
Strengths:
- strongest balance of readable full name and proprietary geometry;
- intervention is concentrated enough to become recognizable without fragmenting the whole word;
- works with the strategy that the word itself should carry the brand;
- plausible path to a cleaner final drawing.

Risks:
- the S/P relationship must be redrawn carefully; current exploration is too stylized to use as production artwork;
- avoid drifting into motorsport/game typography.

Decision: advance as primary finalist.

### A2 — REJECT
The P/L step is visually clever but creates unnecessary interruption and weaker reading rhythm. It demonstrates the concept rather than embodying it naturally.

### A3 — KEEP / secondary finalist
Strengths:
- central L/I coupling gives the word a controlled focal point;
- less aggressive than A1;
- potentially more durable and industrial if simplified.

Risks:
- central notch can read as damage/artifact at small size;
- needs a much subtler production treatment.

Decision: advance as secondary finalist.

### A4 — REJECT
The intervention around I/N harms legibility and adds an arbitrary speed/dynamic cue. Too close to generic performance typography.

### A5 — REJECT
The continuous groove idea is conceptually relevant but visually weak in the current word. It risks becoming an unexplained decorative cut rather than a distinctive identity asset.

### A6 — REJECT
The central coupling is too small to create meaningful distinctiveness at normal/mobile sizes and too easy to disappear in reproduction.

## C-series — symbol elimination

### C1 — HOLD, not approved
Most promising of the symbol set because it is simple and can express two coupled forms. However it resembles the broad family of stylized S / chain / interlock marks used across technology, industrial and sports brands. It requires a proper similarity screen and stronger proprietary construction before use.

### C2 — REJECT
Hexagonal enclosure immediately moves toward generic engineering/crypto/industrial-logo territory. Conflicts with the anti-generic rules.

### C3 — REJECT
Too close to a generic rounded S/interlock app icon. Weak category ownership.

### C4 — REJECT
Mechanical coupling is visible, but the shape is less clearly an S and becomes an abstract industrial icon without sufficient brand ownership.

### C5 — REJECT
Diagonal construction introduces speed/performance styling that is not required by Spline's actual positioning.

### C6 — REJECT
Circle + S is highly conventional and too easy to confuse with existing marks. It adds a badge without adding distinctive meaning.

## Symbol decision

Do **not** make a standalone symbol mandatory for V1.

Spline is a short six-letter name. The current evidence favors building recognition around a proprietary wordmark first. A compact symbol should be derived only after the final wordmark has stable geometry; ideally it should inherit a distinctive construction rule from the wordmark rather than being designed independently.

This follows the stronger principle seen in Vroom: do not add automotive cleverness when the name can carry the identity, and Bosch/Brembo's emphasis on simplicity and small-size performance.

## Finalists

### Finalist 1 — A1 / Interlock at S–P
Recommended leader.

Development target:
- preserve immediate reading as SPLINE;
- reduce futuristic styling;
- make the S/P coupling feel machined/intentional, not decorative;
- normalize the remaining letters so the intervention has contrast;
- test at mobile-header size and monochrome print size.

### Finalist 2 — A3 / Central L–I coupling
Recommended challenger.

Development target:
- simplify the central joint dramatically;
- ensure L and I remain unambiguous;
- use quieter, more industrial proportions than the exploration rendering;
- compare whether the central coupling is more ownable than A1's initial-letter coupling.

## Decision gate

Next round should NOT create another broad sheet of logo ideas.

Next task:
1. reconstruct A1 and A3 as clean vector-like wordmark specifications;
2. define base proportions, stroke/weight, spacing and exact coupling geometry;
3. test both at realistic sizes: 140–180 px website header, small social avatar context, monochrome invoice/sticker;
4. run a visual similarity screen focused on the distinctive coupling treatment;
5. choose one wordmark architecture;
6. only then derive compact mark/avatar behavior from the winning geometry.

No color, gradients, mockup effects or automotive pictograms until the wordmark architecture passes this gate.

## Current recommendation

**A1 leads. A3 remains the challenger. Standalone C-symbol development is paused.**

This is an architecture decision, not approval of the generated A1 artwork as a finished logo.
