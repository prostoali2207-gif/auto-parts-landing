# Spline — Visual Language & Photography System

Status: approved direction for V1 brand identity.

This document extends the approved Spline wordmark, color system and typography. It does not change landing-page structure or CRO.

## Core visual idea

**PRECISION / FIT / CONNECTION**

Spline should look like a business that identifies the correct component and connects a customer request to the right part. The visual language comes from mechanical fit, indexing, alignment and part identification — not from generic racing imagery.

The identity should feel:
- precise;
- mechanical;
- practical;
- controlled;
- modern;
- credible.

It should not feel:
- luxury-car lifestyle;
- motorsport/tuning;
- gaming;
- SaaS/3D software;
- generic industrial stock photography.

## Graphic language

### 1. Alignment marks
Use restrained registration/indexing marks inspired by technical identification systems:
- short ticks;
- datum/alignment lines;
- numbered references;
- small coordinate-like labels.

They are supporting devices, never decoration covering the whole page.

### 2. Connection motif
The `SP` interlock in the wordmark is the source of the brand gesture.

Supporting graphics may use:
- two edges meeting precisely;
- paired slots/notches;
- aligned interruptions in a rule;
- matched positive/negative shapes.

Do not copy the logo geometry repeatedly as a pattern. The principle is connection, not logo wallpaper.

### 3. Rules and frames
Prefer:
- thin straight rules;
- open frames;
- hard rectangular crops;
- asymmetric technical layouts;
- clear spacing.

Avoid default UI-card styling, excessive rounded rectangles, glassmorphism and decorative shadows.

### 4. Numbering
IBM Plex Mono may carry small identifiers such as:
- `01 / VEHICLE`;
- `02 / PART`;
- `OEM`;
- `VIN`;
- request/reference numbers.

Numbers should make information feel traceable, not imitate a fake dashboard.

### 5. Signal orange
`#E85D24` acts as a marker/highlighter:
- one selected item;
- one CTA;
- a small indexing mark;
- a request/reference highlight.

Do not use large orange decorative fields unless there is a functional reason.

## Photography direction

Photography must provide evidence of the actual parts business. Real business imagery is preferred over stock.

### Priority subjects
1. Real auto parts photographed clearly.
2. OEM/part-number labels and packaging where customer/supplier-sensitive data is safe to show.
3. Detail close-ups: connectors, mounting points, teeth, housings, machined surfaces, seals, fasteners.
4. Part-in-hand / inspection moments without staged lifestyle posing.
5. Shelving, boxes, sourcing/receiving context when genuinely belonging to the business.
6. Vehicle context only when it helps identify fitment or the request.

### Composition
Prefer:
- tight or medium crops;
- one clear subject;
- visible material texture;
- neutral perspective or deliberate technical angle;
- negative space when text must coexist;
- hard crop edges.

Avoid:
- generic supercar hero shots;
- dramatic drifting/racing images;
- fake workshop scenes;
- models posing with cars;
- excessive shallow-depth glamour photography;
- AI-generated parts presented as real inventory;
- imagery that implies stock, suppliers or facilities the business does not actually have.

### Light and treatment
Aim for honest, controlled light:
- neutral daylight or soft workshop light;
- preserve metal/plastic/rubber texture;
- moderate contrast;
- no teal/orange cinematic grade;
- no fake chrome glow;
- no heavy HDR;
- no unnecessary monochrome filter.

The warm `Spline Paper` UI can surround photography, but photographs themselves should retain believable color.

## Image hierarchy

Use imagery only when it adds evidence or comprehension.

Preferred order:
1. real business proof;
2. useful part/detail identification;
3. contextual vehicle image;
4. abstract/decorative imagery — normally omit.

If no credible real image exists for a section, whitespace and typography are better than filler stock.

## Social / avatar treatment

The full SPLINE wordmark remains the primary brand asset.

For profile avatars where the full wordmark becomes unreadable:
- do not invent a permanent standalone `S` logo yet;
- use a simple temporary crop/treatment derived from the approved wordmark only after checking legibility;
- otherwise use the full wordmark on a plain `Spline Paper` or `Spline Ink` field.

A standalone symbol becomes a separate design task only if actual channel constraints prove it necessary.

## Brand applications

### Landing page
Use:
- full wordmark;
- paper/ink foundation;
- signal orange sparingly;
- IBM Plex Sans + Mono roles;
- technical rules/index marks where they support structure;
- real parts photography when available.

Do not change page structure merely to display brand decoration.

### Instagram / Telegram
Keep templates simple:
- wordmark;
- one strong real image;
- short headline;
- small technical identifier when relevant;
- one signal-orange accent maximum.

Avoid poster-like layers of badges, stickers, gradients and automotive effects.

### Documents / quotes / request sheets
Use the identity conservatively:
- wordmark top-left;
- clear reference/request number;
- monochrome-first layout;
- orange only for a small status/reference accent;
- strong information hierarchy over decoration.

### Packaging / stickers — future
If introduced later, prioritize:
- wordmark;
- reference/OEM/request data;
- high legibility;
- durable one- or two-color printing.

Do not design packaging systems before the business actually needs them.

## Non-negotiable exclusions

Do not introduce:
- gear/cog logos;
- car silhouettes;
- wings;
- shields;
- speed lines;
- checkered flags;
- carbon-fibre textures;
- chrome/metallic logo effects;
- neon glow;
- generic red/black tuning palette;
- fake CAD drawings used only as decoration;
- repeating spline-shaft patterns everywhere;
- AI-generated fake inventory or facilities.

## Production test

Any new branded visual should pass four questions:
1. Is it recognizably consistent with Spline without relying on automotive clichés?
2. Does it communicate precision/fit/identification or provide real evidence?
3. Is it simpler than the alternative?
4. Would removing it make the communication worse? If not, remove it.
