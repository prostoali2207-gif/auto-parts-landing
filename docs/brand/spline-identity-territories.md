# Spline — Brand Identity Exploration

Status: exploration brief, not final logo.

## Brand decision
Working final brand: **Spline**.
Naming verdict: KEEP WITH CAUTION. Do not reopen naming during identity exploration unless a material UAE trademark/legal conflict appears.

Business: UAE automotive parts request/sourcing business.
Primary early touchpoints: mobile landing page, Instagram, WhatsApp/Telegram, CRM/invoices, labels/stickers.

## Evidence-led brand idea
The strongest authentic automotive meaning of `spline` is mechanical: matching ridges/teeth on a shaft and mating component transmit torque while maintaining alignment. This gives the brand a real engineering concept: **fit / interface / transfer / precision / connection**.

Important: this meaning is a source of design logic, not permission to draw a literal gear, shaft or transmission component.

Reference lesson from Bosch: its mark was deliberately simple enough to engrave on very small products/spare parts, distinctive, internationally usable, and memorable. For Spline, small-size/monochrome performance is therefore a first-order requirement, not a late mockup check.

Search-conflict constraint: `Spline` is also a prominent 3D/web design brand. The automotive identity must avoid looking like a software/creative-tool startup: no soft 3D blobs, playful gradient-first identity, generic tech-SaaS geometry, or identity dependent on interactive 3D effects.

## What Spline must signal
- competent automotive sourcing;
- precision without pretending to be an OEM/manufacturer;
- ability to identify/match the correct part;
- credible modern UAE business;
- enough breadth to expand beyond the current social-selling model.

It must NOT imply unsupported German heritage, motorsport pedigree, luxury positioning, OEM affiliation, guaranteed fitment, or manufacturing capability.

## Architecture decision
Explore the **full SPLINE wordmark first**. The brand is young and the name itself carries the useful mechanical association; hiding it behind an abstract icon would waste recognition.

A compact symbol may be derived later only if the wordmark produces a genuinely ownable construction. Do not begin with an `S` badge.

## Territory A — Interlock Wordmark [PRIMARY]
Core idea: two forms meet precisely, derived abstractly from the male/female relationship of a spline connection.

Architecture: custom uppercase `SPLINE` wordmark.

Construction direction:
- restrained engineered grotesk proportions;
- one controlled letter interaction or cut/terminal system that expresses mating/alignment;
- investigate `P/L`, `L/I`, or repeated terminal geometry before touching the `S`;
- preserve immediate reading at mobile-header size;
- geometry must work in one color.

Why it fits: converts the actual mechanical meaning of the name into a visual rule without drawing a car part.

Risk: over-design can make the word look like a tuning/performance logo. Avoid aggressive italics, speed cuts and excessive angularity.

## Territory B — Indexed Precision
Core idea: spline teeth distribute load through repeated, accurately indexed contact points.

Architecture: wordmark plus a minimal repeatable indexing motif that can later become a secondary brand device.

Construction direction:
- clean wordmark remains dominant;
- establish a small repeat/spacing system inspired by equally indexed contact, not literal teeth;
- motif can support labels, dividers, social templates and packaging without becoming a decorative pattern everywhere;
- symbol only if the motif survives at favicon/avatar scale.

Why it fits: creates a broader identity system, not merely a logo.

Risk: repeated bars/notches can resemble generic industrial or barcode graphics. Distinctive proportion and relationship to the wordmark are mandatory.

## Territory C — Coupled S
Core idea: derive a compact mark from two mating surfaces while retaining an unmistakable `S` relationship.

Architecture: custom wordmark + optional compact mark.

Construction direction:
- develop the wordmark first;
- derive the compact form from the same cuts/radii/spacing rules;
- compact mark must read as Spline's asset, not a generic hexagon, chain link, infinity sign or crypto/tech icon;
- no enclosing shield/circle unless functionally required later.

Why it fits: potentially solves Instagram avatar/favicon/signage use while maintaining one construction language.

Risk: highest cliché/similarity risk of the three. Kill this territory if the symbol becomes more generic than the wordmark.

## Territory D — Parts Label / Technical Identification [SECONDARY SYSTEM, NOT PRIMARY LOGO]
Core idea: Spline behaves visually like a precise, trustworthy parts-identification system.

Architecture: restrained wordmark supported by information typography, codes, rules and structured labels.

Use: request confirmations, packaging/stickers, social proof/order graphics, invoices and future catalog/part pages.

Why it fits: directly supports the business task of identifying and sourcing parts.

Risk: can become cold or fake-industrial if filled with invented part numbers/specifications. Never fabricate technical data merely for aesthetics.

## Recommended exploration order
1. **A — Interlock Wordmark**: strongest combination of meaning, recognition and low implementation complexity.
2. **B — Indexed Precision**: strongest candidate for a scalable identity system around the wordmark.
3. **C — Coupled S**: explore only after A/B establish proprietary geometry.
4. **D — Technical Identification**: use as supporting system after logo direction is selected.

## Anti-directions
Reject:
- literal spline shaft/gear illustration;
- car silhouette;
- steering wheel/speedometer;
- shield/badge;
- wings/crown;
- hexagon containing `S`;
- generic italic racing font;
- red/black tuning-shop default used without evidence;
- chrome/metallic 3D logo;
- gradient-first tech/SaaS treatment;
- soft 3D visual language that could be confused with Spline (3D design software);
- arbitrary cuts in every letter merely to look automotive.

## First visual round requirements
Produce only black/white concept sheets first.

For A, B and C show:
- primary wordmark at normal size;
- wordmark at realistic mobile-header size;
- monochrome reverse version;
- compact/avatar treatment only where justified;
- one construction close-up explaining the proprietary geometry.

No vehicle mockups, neon walls, metallic embossing or dramatic presentation renders in round one. They obscure weak logo geometry.

## Selection gate
A direction advances only if it passes all of these:
- `SPLINE` reads immediately;
- does not look like a generic garage/tuning logo;
- does not look like a 3D/SaaS design tool;
- works in pure black/white;
- survives mobile header and small label use;
- has a repeatable construction rule rather than random decoration;
- can extend into the landing page without forcing a redesign of the conversion UX;
- no obvious confusing similarity to a major automotive mark.

## Next action
Create visual Round 1 for **A / B / C only**. Do not pick colors yet. Compare the three side-by-side, kill weak territories, then refine the winner into the responsive logo system.
