# Spline — A1 vs A3 refinement

Status: refinement gate before final logo construction.

## Decision context
The exploration round retained A1 and A3 as the only wordmark directions worth developing. No standalone S symbol is approved. The brand must work primarily as a readable `SPLINE` wordmark and communicate precise fit/connection without becoming a generic tuning, racing, software, or industrial-CAD identity.

This round is intentionally about construction, not generating more concepts.

## Shared requirements
Both finalists must preserve:
- immediate reading as `SPLINE`;
- monochrome recognition;
- no dependence on gradients, chrome, speed lines, shields, gears, hexagons, or automotive clichés;
- no standalone symbol unless later evidence proves one is needed;
- one controlled intervention maximum should carry the connection/fit idea;
- supporting letters must remain quieter than the distinctive intervention;
- compact horizontal proportions suitable for a mobile website header;
- robust counters and gaps that survive reduction.

## A1 — Interlock wordmark

### What is worth preserving
A1 has the stronger brand idea: connection is embedded directly into the name instead of appended as decoration. It has better potential to make `SPLINE` ownable without requiring an icon.

### Refinement target
Reduce the visual novelty until the word reads first and the interlock is discovered second.

### Construction rules
1. Use one primary connection event, preferably around the opening `S/P` relationship if it can remain unmistakably legible.
2. Do not make every letter mechanically modified.
3. Keep the `S` unmistakably an S; avoid shapes that read as a chain link, infinity mark, or generic motorsport stripe.
4. Keep the `P` bowl and stem sufficiently conventional to avoid `R`, `D`, or abstract-symbol readings.
5. `L I N E` should establish rhythm and stability rather than compete with the opening feature.
6. The interlock gap must remain visibly open at small size; do not depend on hairline negative space.
7. Avoid excessive horizontal extension: the mark must fit comfortably in a mobile header beside navigation/menu controls.
8. Optical spacing overrides mathematical spacing. In particular inspect `SP`, `PL`, `LI`, `IN`, `NE` as visual pairs.

### Failure conditions
Reject A1 if:
- `SP` becomes a symbol before it becomes letters;
- the mark resembles a racing/tuning logo;
- reduction closes the connection gap;
- the custom treatment makes the rest of the word feel like a generic font attached to an icon.

## A3 — Restrained precision wordmark

### What is worth preserving
A3 is calmer, more credible and easier to deploy. It has lower novelty but lower risk. It can support a business that needs trust before visual theatre.

### Refinement target
Create distinctiveness through proportion, spacing and one restrained fit/joint detail rather than an obvious graphic trick.

### Construction rules
1. Preserve straightforward reading at first glance.
2. Use a single subtle joint/notch/alignment intervention; the detail should reward closer inspection rather than interrupt reading.
3. Avoid literal gear teeth, spline-shaft drawings or engineering-diagram decoration inside the letters.
4. Establish a consistent stroke system and corner logic across the word.
5. Keep `I` from disappearing at small sizes.
6. Ensure `N` does not become visually dominant through diagonal weight.
7. Use custom spacing deliberately; an untouched off-the-shelf font is not an acceptable final logo.

### Failure conditions
Reject A3 if:
- after removing the small intervention it is indistinguishable from a stock geometric sans;
- the intervention looks like damage/glitch rather than fit/alignment;
- the identity becomes generic industrial software/CAD branding.

## Small-size gate
Before choosing a winner, both constructions must be checked conceptually at these approximate rendered wordmark heights:
- 48 px — large mobile/header use;
- 32 px — normal header use;
- 24 px — constrained navigation use;
- 16 px — stress test only, not necessarily a required production size.

At each size check:
- SPLINE is readable without context;
- S and P do not merge;
- I remains visible;
- negative-space joints stay open;
- no feature becomes accidental visual noise.

If a detail fails below 24–32 px, simplify the detail rather than creating a separate micro-logo prematurely.

## Monochrome gate
Test black on white and white on black before introducing brand color. Reverse usage must not close counters or make the interlock appear heavier than the rest of the word.

## Comparative evaluation

| Criterion | A1 | A3 |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate readability | Good, requires refinement | Strong |
| Distinctiveness potential | Strong | Medium |
| Ownable brand idea | Strong | Medium |
| Small-size risk | Medium | Low |
| Generic automotive risk | Medium if over-styled | Low |
| Generic tech/industrial risk | Low–medium | Medium |
| Need for separate icon | Low | Low |
| Best role | Primary candidate | Control / fallback |

## Current recommendation
**A1 remains the primary direction. A3 remains the control.**

A1 should win only if its connection feature can be simplified enough that `SPLINE` remains effortless to read at mobile-header size. If that cannot be achieved, choose A3 rather than forcing the concept.

Do not create a standalone S icon during this refinement round. The wordmark itself is the identity asset.

## Next gate
The next work item is not another ideation board. It is a production-oriented construction pass for A1 and A3 against these rules, followed by one final selection.

Only after a winner is selected should the project define:
- exact vector geometry;
- clear space;
- minimum production size;
- reverse version;
- brand color;
- supporting typography;
- website/header asset handoff.
