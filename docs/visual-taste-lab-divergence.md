# Spline Visual Taste Lab — Clean-Slate Divergence

Status: EXPERIMENT ONLY. Do not merge into production.

## Baseline rule
Current production V6 is used only as a comparison baseline. Its visual language is forbidden as an input to the new concepts.

Do not reuse: THE UNBOXING, package/shipping-label composition, routing tape, warm paper + safety orange balance, current hero geometry, current process numerals, or manifest/work-surface metaphor.

Preserve only business truth and conversion constraints: Spline sources auto parts in the UAE; primary action is Request a Part; users may identify vehicle by VIN OR make+model+year; part signal may be name, OEM/part number, description, or photo; contact is required; no fabricated proof.

## External reference mechanisms reviewed
Official rendered pages reviewed across automotive, industrial/product, editorial and retail: Bosch Mobility Aftermarket, Bosch parts catalogue, Porsche model overview, Teenage Engineering OP-1 field, Teenage Engineering Field System, Nothing, RIMOWA, Aesop, Leica, and Monocle.

Mechanisms extracted rather than copied:
- product/object scale as composition;
- editorial density changes rather than repeated card bands;
- large calm image/shape fields;
- strong black/white value hierarchy;
- branded typography carrying identity;
- small technical detail used sparingly;
- navigation and CTA embedded into the composition rather than decorated afterward;
- full-page rhythm switching between loud and quiet acts.

## 18 concept divergence
1. Exploded Precision — abstract exploded mechanical geometry, clinical white/graphite/acid yellow.
2. Workshop Editorial — service as a serious magazine spread, oxblood/ivory/black.
3. Cutaway Index — sectional planes and cutaway windows; rejected as too close to technical diagram language.
4. Inventory Silence — near-empty gallery with one giant object silhouette; commercially too quiet.
5. Service Newspaper — dense broadsheet with strong columns; merged into Workshop Editorial.
6. Black Room — full black cinematic typography; too luxury/performance-coded.
7. Foundry Sheet — metallic gray planes and stamped type; visually strong but risked industrial B2B heaviness.
8. Parts Constellation — floating components as abstract stars; too decorative.
9. Calibration Field — sparse measurement grid; rejected for workstation drift.
10. Street Poster — cultural poster / flyposting energy; strong magnetism, weaker trust for the service.
11. Archive Drawer — archival folders and catalog tabs; too close to packaging/identifier family.
12. Material Slice — layered physical cross-sections; promising but secondary to Exploded Precision.
13. Typographic Collision — almost no imagery, giant words colliding with form; strong but less automotive specificity.
14. Service Atelier — fashion-house calm with close-up material surfaces; merged into Workshop Editorial.
15. One Red Object — monochrome layout with one saturated object field; strong mechanism, became part of Editorial.
16. Blueprint Reversal — white marks on cobalt; rejected because technical-blueprint cliché.
17. Modular Sculpture — abstract machine assembled from rectangles/circles; merged into Exploded Precision.
18. Wildcard: Parts Radio — interface as broadcast frequencies; memorable but semantic mismatch.

## Radical shortlist
### A — Exploded Precision
Thesis: Spline is the moment a vague problem becomes a precise mechanical target.
Composition: enormous sans typography counterweighted by an abstract exploded mechanism. White dominates, graphite grounds, fluorescent yellow marks only action/selection.
Signature moment: the hero mechanical form appears to separate into identifiable layers without pretending to be a real part.
Commercial advantage: immediate automotive/mechanical energy without car imagery, dashboard UI, or logistics metaphor.
Risk: can become cold or engineering-software-like if micro labels proliferate.

### B — Workshop Editorial
Thesis: sourcing a part is expert editorial judgment, not browsing a catalogue.
Composition: magazine-cover hero, serif/sans contrast, oxblood field, ivory reading surface, sharp black rules, deliberately asymmetric columns.
Signature moment: the request invitation behaves like a full editorial chapter opener rather than a form container.
Commercial advantage: trust, maturity, distinctiveness, strong reading rhythm, no fake proof required.
Risk: can feel fashion/editorial rather than automotive if process language is too abstract.

### C — Street Service Poster
Thesis: one urgent message, one service action, zero catalogue friction.
Composition: poster-scale type, fluorescent fields, compressed vertical rhythm, almost no conventional sections.
Commercial advantage: exceptional mobile magnetism.
Risk: perceived quality and trust below A/B for the UAE parts-sourcing context.

## Selection for web prototypes
Build A and B. They are materially different from each other and from production V6, while retaining clear commercial behavior.

The prototypes are visual-only lab routes. They must not call CRM or analytics and must carry no fabricated evidence.
